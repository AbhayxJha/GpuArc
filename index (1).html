01The problem

AI chips are getting too hot for today's cooling

Each new GPU generation pushes more power through a similar footprint. Most liquid cooling still uses a cold plate bonded to the chip, which adds thermal layers between the silicon and the coolant.

02The idea

Spray the chip. Let gravity do the drainage.

The GPU is mounted face-down in a sealed chamber. A nozzle sprays coolant directly onto the die. Heated coolant runs down the walls into a small sump, then loops back through a pump and chiller.

03How it works

Three steps in a closed loop

STEP 1

Spray

The nozzle directs coolant onto the face-down die.

STEP 2

Drain

Heated coolant returns along the walls into the sump.

STEP 3

Recirculate

A pump moves it through a chiller and back to the nozzle.

STATUS
Concept-stage. CAD in progress. No physical prototype. No experimental validation yet. All figures below are analytical estimates from first-principles heat transfer, not measured results.

Concept

Gravity-assisted direct spray cooling for AI GPUs. The chip is mounted face-down in a sealed chamber. Coolant is sprayed onto the die, drains by gravity to a sump, and is recirculated through an external chiller. This removes the bonded cold plate and its thermal interface layers.

Baseline assumptions

REFERENCE CHIP

H100-class

700 W, die area ~814 mm² (8.14 cm²), heat flux ~86 W/cm²

COOLANT

Deionized (DI) water

Inlet 45 °C, coolant rise 10 K

LIMITS

Max junction 85 °C

Pump efficiency 50%, spray pressure 5 bar

Key engineering challenges (open)

  1. Electrical protection: DI water contacting a powered die requires a protective barrier or coating, which adds thermal resistance and must be validated.
  2. Spray coverage: achieving h ≥ ~21,500 W/m²K with uniform spray coverage across the die.
  3. Pump penalty: pump pressure and power penalty in direct-to-chip spray configurations.
  4. Die and lid handling: exposing the die for direct spray.

05Prior art

Built on a known approach, with a different goal

Spray cooling is not new. The closest prior art is the ISR/SprayCool patent (US 6,889,515), with a similar chip-down, spray-up, gravity-drain geometry. GpuArc revisits it for modern AI GPU power levels.
